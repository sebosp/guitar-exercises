# Gentle error signaling in real-time practice apps (tuners, rhythm games, learning apps)

Scope: how to tell a player "off target" (sharp/flat, early/late) and "the app adjusted something because of misses" without punishing them. Target: a guitar trainer with a metronome and microphone pitch detection. Research done 2026-11-03; ~37 web calls. Findings tagged **[study]**, **[official guidance]**, **[product doc]**, **[anecdote/forum]**, **[secondary]**.

## Key question 1: What do HCI, game UX and education research say about punishing vs informative feedback, learned helplessness and error framing?

### Takeaway
The consistent, evidence-backed guidance is: describe the problem precisely, give the correction, don't blame, avoid loaded words ("invalid", "wrong"), and keep the treatment proportional to severity. The dramatic claims (red hurts performance, growth-mindset framing transforms outcomes) are weakly supported or failed replication; the robust findings are about controllability (helplessness comes from uncontrollable outcomes, not from being told you missed) and about communication of failure (Juul: the issue is how failure is communicated, not how often it happens). Real-time feedback also carries a motor-learning risk (guidance hypothesis): constant concurrent feedback can create dependency, so it should be reducible/fadeable.

### Cited Findings

**Error message design (HCI, official guidance)**
- NN/g error-message guidelines: place the message next to the element that caused the error (proximity); use noticeable, accessible indicators (bold, high-contrast red text plus highlights on the affected element) but "never rely on color or animation alone" (about 350 million people have colour-vision deficiency); match treatment to severity ("inline labels, toasts, or banners for minor issues", modal dialogs only for severe errors needing resolution); don't show errors too early (an empty field is exploration, not an error); use plain language, describe the exact problem, offer remedies; "keep the tone positive and nonjudgmental", avoid "invalid", "illegal", "incorrect", never shift blame; avoid humour because it goes stale on repetition. **[official guidance]** — [NN/g Error-Message Guidelines](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/error-message-guidelines/)
- NN/g video summary of the four communication guidelines: human-readable language, concise and precise description, constructive advice, no blame. **[official guidance]** — [NN/g video](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/error-message-communication-guidelines/)
- NN/g also ties catastrophic-failure handling to negativity bias and the peak-end rule (bad moments are remembered disproportionately, and the end of a session colours the memory of the whole). **[official guidance]** — [NN/g Error-Message Guidelines](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/error-message-guidelines/)
- Google's technical-writing guide on error messages: avoid relying solely on colour to highlight errors; add boldface, spacing or underlining. **[official guidance]** — [Google tech writing: format error messages](https://developers.google.com/tech-writing/error-messages/format-for-readability)
- WCAG 1.4.1 "Use of Color": a red border alone is not sufficient for an invalid state; add a message. **[official guidance, summarised]** — [WCAG 1.4.1 toolkit summary](https://www.disabilityworld.org/toolkit/standards/wcag/1-4-1-use-of-color/)

**Game UX on failure**
- Juul's 2009 GDC talk abstract ("Beyond Balancing: Using Five Elements of Failure Design"): player research shows "the primary issue is not the frequency of failures, but how failure is communicated, what happens as a result of failing, and whether a given failure design allows the game to be enjoyed within a player's time constraints"; casual players are not averse to failure. **[designer research/talk]** — [Juul, GDC 2009 post](https://www.jesperjuul.net/ludologist/?p=590)
- Juul, *The Art of Failure*: failure is sorted on attribution axes internal/external (player's fault vs game's), stable/unstable (skill vs chance), global/specific. **[book, via review]** — [Liz England review](https://lizengland.com/blog/review-the-art-of-failure-by-jesper-juul/); the "paradox of failure" (players avoid failure yet seek it) — [MIT Press page](https://www.jesperjuul.net/artoffailure)
- An Oregon State teaching blog applying Juul: the combination Internal + Stable + Global failure feedback ("it's you, it won't change, you're bad at everything") "would contribute most strongly toward a player adopting a learned helplessness mindset". **[secondary, interpretive]** — [OSU blog](https://blogs.oregonstate.edu/inspire/?p=3275)
- Apple HIG (games): "when a game displays a succinct animation that's precisely tied to a successful action, players can instantly get the message without being distracted from their gameplay"; brief and precise feedback animation "can often convey information more effectively than prominent animation". **[official guidance]** — [Apple HIG Motion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion)
- Apple HIG (haptics): keep a consistent causal mapping; "if your game plays a specific haptic pattern when a character fails to finish a mission, people associate that pattern with a negative outcome", don't reuse it for positive outcomes. **[official guidance]** — [Apple HIG Playing haptics](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics)

**Learned helplessness and negative feedback**
- Winefield et al.: learned-helplessness deficits are produced by prior exposure to *uncontrollable* outcomes, not by perceived failure; failure feedback alone was not associated with poorer performance. **[study]** — [Flinders record](https://researchnow.flinders.edu.au/en/publications/learned-helplessness-deficits-uncontrollable-outcomes-or-perceive/)
- Foster's dissertation (112 undergraduates): results did not support learned-helplessness theory; earlier effects may have come from believability of the feedback, amount of failure feedback, or fatigue. **[study]** — [UND thesis](https://commons.und.edu/theses/2921)
- Weisz (Harvard): under consistently negative feedback, children with intellectual disabilities showed "striking deterioration in their strategy use" versus a comparison group. **[study]** — [Weisz lab PDF](https://weiszlab.fas.harvard.edu/file_url/335)
- Simulation-game studies using Dweck's framework: performance-oriented students may not exert effort after negative feedback, learning-oriented students may; small samples (n=33) and "relatively little work" on reactions to negative feedback. **[study, small]** — [ABSEL paper](https://absel-ojs-ttu.tdl.org/absel/article/view/502)
- Utrecht thesis: negative performance feedback produced more threat and less challenge appraisal than positive feedback; self-efficacy did not moderate. **[thesis]** — [Utrecht thesis](https://studenttheses.uu.nl/handle/20.500.12932/43893)
- Gardner & Wood (2009): framing errors positively ("learning opportunities") produced more unsystematic exploration and *worse* performance than negative framing on a management task; positive framing helped low-self-efficacy people, negative framing was better for high-self-efficacy people. **[study]** — [Massey record](https://mro.massey.ac.nz/items/e7779b6c-4756-4112-acf1-c2ca9661e564/full)
- Durham string-player study: negative post-event rumination and performance anxiety were strongly positively correlated. **[study]** — [Frontiers 2019](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.01931/pdf)

**Growth mindset / "not yet"**
- Dweck's TED talk: a Chicago high school gives the grade "Not Yet" instead of a fail; the words "yet"/"not yet" "give kids greater confidence, give them a path into the future that creates greater persistence"; praise process (effort, strategy, focus, perseverance, improvement) not talent. **[talk, claim]** — [Transcript (third-party)](https://singjupost.com/carol-s-dweck-the-power-of-yet-at-tedxnorrkoping-transcript/)
- Sisk et al. 2018 (Psychological Science) two meta-analyses: mindset–achievement correlation across k=273, N=365,915 is very weak; intervention effect across k=43, N=57,155 is very small; benefits concentrated in low-SES students; interactive formats helped where passive reading didn't; publication-bias signals. **[meta-analysis]** — [Sisk et al. 2018 PDF](https://hhs.purdue.edu/skill-learning-and-performance-lab/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2024/08/sisk-et-al-2018-to-what-extent-and-under-which-circumstances-are-growth-mind-sets-important-to-academic-achievement-two.pdf); critique that light-touch interventions dominated — [Progress Focused analysis](https://www.progressfocused.com/2018/04/critical-analysis-by-sisk-et-al-2018.html)
- Duolingo "hard exercise" indicator: a small message at the top of the screen flags that an exercise is harder than usual; learners responded very positively (more study time, more likely to return next day); a learner said "If I make a mistake, I don't feel as bad, because you warned me that it was hard. If I get it right, I feel proud." **[product research, no stats]** — [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-difficult-exercises/)

**Red, flashes, shaking**
- Elliot et al. 2007 (6 experiments, 282 students): brief exposure to red before a test lowered performance; interpreted as avoidance motivation. **[study]** — [APA Monitor](https://www.apa.org/monitor/may07/seeingred); [ScienceDaily](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2007/02/070228170240.htm)
- Steele lab replications (Appalachian State): 2015 (n=310), 2016 (higher saturation) and 2017 (n=386) found no significant red effect. **[replication failures]** — [2015 poster](https://www.appstate.edu/~steelekm/documents/Psychonomics2015Poster_Steele.pdf); [2017 poster](https://www.appstate.edu/~steelekm/documents/Psychonomic2017Poster_Steele.pdf)
- No study found measuring screen shake / red flash effects on anxiety or performance specifically. Closest: Nacke et al. 2011 modulated character shake via physiology ("noticed but not too much so that the game could not be played"); Graja, Lopes & Chanel 2021 studied which light/sound effects raise arousal in a horror game. **[adjacent]** — [Psychophysiology in games (Nacke)](https://um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/29716/1/Psychophysiology_in_games_2016.pdf); [Graja et al.](https://hei-lab.ulusofona.pt/files/pdfs/Impact_of_Visual_and_Sound_Orchestration_Horror_Game.pdf)
- WCAG 2.3.1 (Level A): nothing flashes more than three times in any one-second period, or stays below the general and red-flash thresholds; the red-flash threshold is stricter. Related criteria 2.2.2 Pause/Stop/Hide and 2.3.3 Animation from Interactions (AAA). Recommendation: fades/transitions rather than strobes. **[official guidance via summaries]** — [wcag.dock.codes](https://wcag.dock.codes/documentation/wcag232); [Yale animated content](https://usability.yale.edu/digital-accessibility/accessibility-resources/accessibility-articles/animated-content-and-timing)

**Motor learning: concurrent feedback dependency**
- Guidance hypothesis: "permanent feedback during acquisition leads to a dependency on the feedback"; learners rely on the augmented signal and neglect intrinsic sensory feedback; reducing feedback frequency lets them build an independent representation (Kovacs et al. bimanual task). **[study/review]** — [Psychological Research review](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s00426-016-0775-0); [Frontiers 2016](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2016.00251/pdf)
- Limits: the hypothesis "is not a general principle"; sonification (auditory concurrent feedback) may escape it; concurrent feedback is less effective for simple tasks than for complex ones. **[review]** — [Sigrist et al. 2013](https://www.doi.org/10.3758/S13423-012-0333-8); [rowing/sonification](https://doc.rero.ch/record/333015/files/221_2014_Article_4167.pdf)
- Real-time intonation feedback, violin beginners (Pardue & McPherson 2019, n=12, four lesson types): visual display = nearest note name plus a bar whose position, colour and size gave direction and size of the error (small green bar = close, tall red bar = far); mean absolute error 17.93 c (none), 18.12 (visual), 18.59 (both), 19.81 (aural), no significant difference, high variance; 10/12 said visual helped them know if in tune; visual "flickered and changed too fast to act on" and gaze data showed students looked at it only 16% of the time; one adult said hearing the correct note made them stop correcting; the authors ask openly whether an "impolite" error highlight would motivate correction or make students avoid the aid. **[study, small]** — [Frontiers in Psychology 2019](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.00627/full)
- Singing: beginners benefit more from real-time pitch feedback than advanced singers, and results "tended to worsen at the moment of receiving feedback"; a 10-week melody-singing study found no significant difference vs control. **[study]** — [Frontiers 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.684693/pdf)
- Middle-school winds (dissertation): on-screen tuner plus exercises and in-tune accompaniment produced a significant intonation gain over control. **[dissertation]** — [Purdue](https://docs.lib.purdue.edu/dissertations/AAI3260001)

### Inferences
- The "punishing" part of feedback that the research actually warns about is attribution (internal+stable+global) and uncontrollability, not the mere fact of flagging a miss. A miss indicator that is specific ("this note, 20 cents sharp") and unstable ("you can fix it now") is informative; a global one ("Failed", streak lost, big red X) is the risky kind.
- The red-colour literature doesn't justify banning red, but combined with NN/g ("never rely on color alone") and WCAG, the safe design is: low-saturation warm colour for "off", a shape/position cue carrying the direction, and no flashing.
- Gardner & Wood suggests that going too far in "mistakes are great!" copy can backfire for confident users; neutral, directional copy is the safer middle.
- Pardue & McPherson's gaze result (16%) and "changed too fast" complaint are the strongest practical findings for a guitar trainer: smooth the pitch readout, hold judgements briefly, and don't demand the eyes during playing.
- Guidance hypothesis: offer a way to reduce feedback frequency (summary after a phrase, or feedback only on larger misses) as the learner improves, rather than constant per-note correction.

### Gaps
- No controlled study found on red flashes, screen shake or buzzers and anxiety/performance in practice apps or rhythm games.
- Hodent's *The Gamer's Brain* was not accessible beyond table-of-contents level; no specific quotes on failure feedback.
- Juul's "five elements of failure" were not retrievable (slides post not fetched).
- Growth-mindset "not yet" copy has no direct A/B evidence in apps beyond Duolingo's descriptive blog post.

## Key question 2: How do tuner apps and pedals show how far sharp/flat and which direction to correct? Metaphors and thresholds

### Takeaway
Three metaphors dominate: needle (centre = in tune, left = flat, right = sharp), strobe (pattern drifts left when flat, right when sharp, stands still when in tune, speed of drift = size of error), and bar/LED ladder with a centre green and red/amber sides. Numeric cents are shown on more precise devices (Peterson 0.1 c, PolyTune needle ±0.5 c / strobe ±0.1 c). Published "in-tune" windows for consumer apps were not found; perceptual data puts the JND around 2–6 cents for trained listeners, and ±5 cents is the forum-folk threshold for "noticeably out".

### Cited Findings
- Peterson strobe: "the strobe image appears to travel to the left if your instrument is flat of the desired pitch and to the right if it is sharp"; adjust until the pattern is "as close to still as possible"; accuracy 0.1 cent (1/1000 semitone); because pitch is "never totally stable" the display needs very fine adjustment before it stops; older Peterson models move up when sharp, down when flat. Some models (tap-tuning mode on StroboSoft) show a numeric cents offset. **[product doc]** — [Peterson StroboSoft FAQ](https://www.petersontuners.com/support/stroboSoft); older Model 400: sharp/flat signs above the disc show direction — [Peterson 400 manual](https://www.petersontuners.com/media/pdf/400INS.pdf)
- Peterson StroboStomp HD reviewer: the pedal does not display the tuning error in cents (strobe only). **[review]** — [Reverb listing/review](https://reverb.com/item/100527369-peterson-strobostomp-hd-tuner)
- TC Electronic PolyTune: needle mode shows note name, "all the needle LEDs are green in the centre 'in tune' position and mainly red if you're out of tune"; needle mode ±0.5 cent, strobe mode ±0.1 cent; polyphonic mode about ±1 cent. On some models the needle itself turns red when sharp/flat and green when correct. **[review of product]** — [Sound On Sound PolyTune review](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/tc-electronic-polytune); [SOS PolyTune 2 Mini](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/tc-electronic-polytune-2-mini); PolyTune Clip ±0.02 c strobe, ±0.5 c chromatic — [Chicago Music Exchange listing](https://www.chicagomusicexchange.com/products/tc-electronic-polytune-clip-17564)
- General hardware convention (Thomann guide): error "usually displayed in cents"; "a central green LED indicates correct tuning, with red LEDs for sharp or flat". **[retailer guide]** — [Thomann tuner display types](https://www.thomannmusic.com/onlineexpert_page_tuners_display_types.html)
- Wikipedia electronic tuner: with needle displays the note is in tune at the 90° vertical position; leftward = flat, rightward = sharp. **[secondary]** — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_tuner)
- Fender FT-1 Pro clip-on: screen backlight turns green when the note is in tune. **[product doc]** — [Fender FT-1 Pro](https://www.fender.com/products/ft-1-pro-clip-on-tuner)
- Apple GarageBand tuner: needle and note name appear green when in tune and orange when flat or sharp (orange, not red). Logic Pro's tuner has a "Tune Deviation" display in cents. **[product doc]** — [GarageBand help](https://help.apple.com/garageband/mac/10.1.5/en.lproj/gbndb40183f2.html); [Logic Pro tuner](https://support.apple.com/it-ch/guide/logicpro/lgcef24f3d8e/mac)
- GuitarTuna: App Store copy promises "instant visual feedback"; a third-party guide says the needle centred and/or the indicator turning green means the string is tuned, and the display tells you "whether you need to tighten or loosen the tuning peg". No official cents tolerance found. **[product listing + secondary]** — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/hk/app/id527588389); [todoandroid guide](https://en.todoandroid.es/guitar-tuna-app-android-guitar-tuner-ukulele-violin-free/)
- Pardue & McPherson violin display (research prototype): note name + a bar whose position, colour and height encode direction and magnitude; small green = close, tall red = far. **[study]** — [Frontiers 2019](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.00627/full)
- Perceptual thresholds: JND about 3 cents for pure tones near 2 kHz, some trained musicians under 2 cents; "about 5–6 cents" as a commonly cited human limit; timbre changes made student musicians miss ±12 cent errors; trained singers typically within 15–20 cents, non-musicians above 50. **[study/secondary]** — [arXiv auditory review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.00084); [Wikipedia Cent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cent_(music)); [NITK singing study](https://idr.nitk.ac.in/handle/123456789/8598)
- Forum folk thresholds: "the ear has difficulty detecting any fault in intonation within 2–3 cents"; "more than five cents between tuners could get you called out of tune". **[anecdote]** — [Luthiers forum](https://www.luthiersforum.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=581663); [UMGF](https://umgf.com/intonation-problems-with-000-15m-i-bought-whose-re-t110763-s20.html)

### Inferences
- A sensible default for a trainer: a horizontal bar/needle with centre = target, a "green" dead zone of about ±5 cents (near the JND for most players, wider than pro tuner precision which would flicker), amber from ±5 to about ±15–20, and the "far" colour beyond; label the direction in words ("sharp / tune down", "flat / tune up") because the needle metaphor alone is ambiguous for beginners (GuitarTuna's third-party guide has to explain which way to turn the peg).
- Strobe-style drift (continuous motion whose speed scales with error) is the most informative display for fine tuning but is also the most "always moving" one; for in-performance pitch feedback a damped needle/bar with hysteresis fits the Pardue & McPherson finding about flicker.
- Orange/amber (GarageBand) rather than red for "off" is an existing precedent in a mainstream product.

### Gaps
- Official GuitarTuna, Fender Tune and Cleartune documentation of their cents windows and display behaviour was not found; Cleartune and Fender Tune returned no usable results at all.
- No official TC Electronic manual was fetched; PolyTune figures come from reviews and listings.
- No published "in-tune window" (e.g. ±3 or ±5 cents) for consumer apps; the figures above are perceptual, not product specs.

## Key question 3: How do rhythm games show early/late without breaking flow? Windows and labels

### Takeaway
The flow-safe pattern is a tiny, transient, directional cue near the hit line (a word like EARLY/LATE, a tilted judgement, or a tick on a hit-error bar), never a modal or a screen-wide effect. Windows are tight: osu!mania PERFECT ±16 ms, GREAT ±64−3·OD ms, MISS beyond ±(188−3·OD) ms. Several games (osu! standard by default, Rhythm Doctor, Rocksmith) are criticised precisely for not telling the player which way they were off.

### Cited Findings
- osu!mania judgement windows (max hit error = half window, ms): PERFECT 16 (fixed), GREAT 64−3·OD, GOOD 97−3·OD, OK 127−3·OD, MEH 151−3·OD, MISS 188−3·OD (OD = overall difficulty 0–10). ScoreV2 PERFECT: 22.4−0.6·OD (OD≤5) or 24.9−1.1·OD (OD≥5). The hit space "is centered around 0 ms offset" with early-miss, early-valid, late-valid and late-miss regions. **[official wiki]** — [osu!mania judgement](https://osu.ppy.sh/wiki/en/Gameplay/Judgement/osu!mania)
- osu! standard: by default "any hit, early or late will be displayed as a 100 or a 50, with no indication of which way you are off, other than the hit accuracy bar at the bottom of the screen"; players requested early/late skins. **[forum]** — [osu! forum](https://osu.ppy.sh/community/forums/posts/2940941)
- osu!mania error meter: splits hits into early (−x ms) and late (+x ms) and averages each side; one description trims the extreme 5% first. **[forum]** — [osu! forum post](https://osu.ppy.sh/community/forums/posts/5704733)
- StepManiaX judgements: PERFECT!!, PERFECT, EARLY, LATE, MISS; "any judgement that is not 'PERFECT!!' will show an indicator if the input was registered early or late". **[secondary]** — [Wikipedia StepManiaX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/StepManiaX)
- Etterna (StepMania engine): configurable error bar; "Error Bar count setting cap raised to 200" in 0.71.2. **[release notes]** — [Etterna 0.71.2](https://forums.etternaonline.com/t/etterna-0-71-2-released/3552)
- Project DIVA Mega Mix+: a subtle indicator tilted left means early, tilted right means late. **[forum]** — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1761390/discussions/0/3422185980116444483)
- Rhythm Doctor: hitting early or late counts as a miss; a 2017 preview said "the biggest flaw right now with Rhythm Doctor is with giving moment-to-moment feedback"; "judgment is too strict" is a common review complaint. **[press/review aggregate]** — [GamingTrend preview](https://gamingtrend.com/friday-night-fibrillating-rhythm-doctor-early-access-preview/); [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/774181/rhythm_doctor)
- Rocksmith 2014: notes "turn 90 degrees at the moment that they should be strummed"; players see an occasional "LATE" pop-up but "MISSED" far more often; "doesn't really outright tell you about your timing and will accept some horrendous timing if the note was right"; groove awards reward consecutive notes slightly ahead, on, or slightly behind the beat. **[secondary/forum]** — [Wikipedia Rocksmith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocksmith); [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/221680/discussions/0/541906989393368380); [AVForums scoring](https://www.avforums.com/threads/rocksmith-scoring.1769460/)
- Rocksmith+ Lessons: feedback on speed and accuracy comes after each Practice Track (summary, not per note). **[official dev diary]** — [Ubisoft dev diary](https://ubisoft.com/en-us/game/rocksmith/plus/news-updates/7ncvKJE9PCWmNWNrqmU5WJ/rs-news-article-dev-diary-for-late-may-2022)
- Third-party "Timing Judge" plugin shows per-note matched/early/late/missed/extra markers (beta). **[product listing]** — [KVR](https://www.kvraudio.com/product/timingjudge-by-timing-judge)
- Clone Hero: notes count when the gem is inside the hit window; the window can be displayed on screen (profile setting); "Drunk Mode" widens it; calibration offsets shift the window for audio/video latency (PC monitor typically 0–5 ms). Exact ms not found. **[official wiki]** — [Clone Hero manual](https://wiki.clonehero.net/books/clone-hero-manual/page/how-to-play); [Calibration](https://wiki.clonehero.net/books/guides-and-tutorials/page/calibrating-audio-and-video)
- Beat Saber: score is 100 pts swing angle + 15 pts cut centrality per block (max 115), "not perfect timing"; the hitbox extends in front of the block so early swings still count; audio-delay setting for latency. No early/late labels. **[wiki/press]** — [BSMG ranking guide](https://bsmg.wiki/ranking-guide); [Road to VR](https://roadtovr.com/beat-saber-studio-shows-get-highest-score-new-video/); [TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/BeatSaber)
- Yousician user reviews complain the app "will say you have missed the note and it will turn red" even when the ball lands on target, and that a miss registers "a couple of nanoseconds after the target". **[anecdote]** — [App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/yousician-learn-play-guitar/id959883039)

### Inferences
- The most-praised designs put the direction in the judgement itself (StepManiaX EARLY/LATE, DIVA's tilt) and keep a cumulative, glanceable hit-error bar; the most-criticised ones give a binary miss with no direction (Rhythm Doctor, osu! default, Rocksmith "MISSED").
- For a metronome trainer, a window structure like osu!mania's (tight "perfect" around ±16–20 ms, graded bands out to ~±150 ms, miss beyond) can be reused with labels, but the labels should be directional and neutral ("early", "late") rather than quality grades ("meh", "bad").
- Calibration/latency offset is a prerequisite: several games expose it because without it the app blames the player for its own latency (the Yousician complaints read like exactly that).

### Gaps
- No design postmortem from Rocksmith or a rhythm-game studio was found on early/late feedback; findings are from wikis, forums and press.
- Guitar Hero / Clone Hero ms windows and Beat Saber ms windows were not found.
- Rhythm Doctor developer statements about its final feedback design were not found (only a 2013 side-project comment).

## Key question 4: How do learning apps show a difficulty adjustment (song slowed, tempo lowered) as a brief non-blocking notice?

### Takeaway
Precedents: Duolingo flags *before* the hard item with a small top-of-screen message (which measurably reduced how bad mistakes felt); Yousician exposes a tempo slider (25–125% in 5% steps) and an "Auto" mode that slows or speeds the song based on performance; Rocksmith's dynamic difficulty levels phrases down after many misses and shows level-change notices for "only a few seconds". Platform guidance says such notices are snackbars/toasts (short, auto-dismiss, one at a time), while anything that must persist should be a banner.

### Cited Findings
- Duolingo: when a learner is doing well the algorithm "selects slightly more challenging exercises from the next skill level"; the team "wanted to tell learners what was happening and why it mattered"; the indicator is "a small message at the top of the screen"; learners spent more time, returned more the next day; quote "If I make a mistake, I don't feel as bad, because you warned me that it was hard." **[product doc]** — [Duolingo difficult exercises](https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-difficult-exercises/); adaptivity keeps "a balance of familiar and more challenging content" — [Duolingo right level of difficulty](https://blog.duolingo.com/right-level-of-difficulty)
- Yousician: Practice mode tempo "works from 25% through to 125%, in 5% increments"; "Auto mode, where Yousician automatically adjusts the song speed — slowing down or speeding up based on how you" play. (Support page itself returned 403; figures from search excerpt of that page.) **[product doc, excerpt]** — [Yousician: best way to practice guitar](https://support.yousician.com/hc/en-us/articles/206912609-The-best-way-to-practice-guitar); singing version — [Yousician singing](https://support.yousician.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000555665-The-best-way-to-practice-singing-in-Yousician)

### Inferences
- Pattern that matches the evidence: (1) a transient chip/snackbar near the tempo readout ("Tempo 80 → 72" or "Slowed to 72 bpm") that auto-dismisses in roughly 3–5 s with a fade, (2) the persistent state carried only by the tempo number itself (and perhaps a small "auto" tag), (3) never a sentence that stays on screen explaining why. If the user can object ("keep tempo"), make it the snackbar's single action and lengthen the display slightly (Material's action-snackbar rule).
- Duolingo's result argues for announcing *before* a harder segment ("faster section ahead") as much as after misses; forewarning reframed mistakes for learners.
- Rocksmith's "Level Up Only" and separate level-down speed are worth copying as settings: let the player decide whether the app may slow them down automatically.
- Material's banner (persistent, needs dismissal) is the wrong primitive for a per-run tempo change; it's for things like "microphone not detected".

## Key question 5: Recommended guidelines for non-aggressive error colour and motion (Material, Apple HIG, NN/g, reduced motion, accessibility)

### Takeaway
Official guidance converges on: motion must be brief, precise, purposeful and optional (Reduce Motion / prefers-reduced-motion replaces motion with fades or colour shifts); colour must be paired with text/icon/shape; no more than three flashes per second and avoid red flashes; haptics complement rather than replace, follow documented meanings (error vs warning vs success), and must be switchable off.

### Cited Findings
- Apple HIG Motion: "Add motion purposefully... Gratuitous or excessive animation can distract people and may make them feel disconnected or physically uncomfortable"; "Make motion optional... avoid using it as the only way to communicate important information... supplement visual feedback by also using alternatives like haptics and audio"; "Aim for brevity and precision in feedback animations"; "generally avoid adding motion to UI interactions that occur frequently"; "Let people cancel motion"; visionOS: avoid motion at the edges of the field of view, avoid sustained oscillation around 0.2 Hz. **[official guidance]** — [Apple HIG Motion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion)
- App Store Connect Reduce Motion criteria: scaling, spinning and peripheral motion are the main problem types; with Reduce Motion on, disable or replace them, and suggested replacements are "a dissolve, highlight fade, or color shift"; stop or give control over ongoing motion. **[official guidance]** — [Reduced Motion evaluation criteria](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-accessibility/reduced-motion-evaluation-criteria)
- Apple HIG Haptics: use system patterns per documented meaning (Notification: Success, Warning, Error; Impact: light/medium/heavy/rigid/soft; Selection); "Avoid overusing haptics... the best haptic experience is one that people may not be conscious of, but miss when it's turned off"; "Make haptics optional"; match haptic intensity/sharpness to the animation; haptic vibration can disrupt "device features like the camera, gyroscope, or microphone". **[official guidance]** — [Apple HIG Playing haptics](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics)
- Android `HapticFeedbackConstants`: `REJECT` (API 30+, value 17) "to signal the rejection or failure of a user interaction"; `CONFIRM` (16) "to signal the confirmation or successful completion of a user interaction"; below API 30 the compat library falls back to LONG_PRESS for REJECT. **[official API]** — [HapticFeedbackConstants](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/view/HapticFeedbackConstants); [HapticFeedbackConstantsCompat](https://developer.android.com/reference/androidx/core/view/HapticFeedbackConstantsCompat)
- NN/g: bold, high-contrast red text and highlights for errors, but "never rely on color or animation alone"; proximity to the element. **[official guidance]** — [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/error-message-guidelines/)
- WCAG: 2.3.1 three flashes / red-flash threshold (Level A); prefers-reduced-motion is a separate user-preference signal to remove or shorten non-essential animation and does not exempt flashing from the limit; parallax and motion can cause dizziness or nausea for vestibular users; prefer CSS transitions/fades to strobes; test with PEAT. **[official guidance via summaries]** — [wcag.dock.codes 2.3.x](https://wcag.dock.codes/documentation/wcag232); [a11y-collective](https://www.a11y-collective.com/blog/wcag-animation/); [UMich vestibular](https://teamdynamix.umich.edu/TDClient/30/Portal/KB/Article/14019/Accommodating-Vertigo-and-Vestibular-Disorders)
- Material error colour: secondary sources cite #B00020 (light) with a lighter dark-mode variant; unverified against the official M3 colour-role page. **[secondary, unverified]** — [Uxcel dark-mode error colours](https://app.uxcel.com/lessons/dark-mode-uncovered-558/error-colors-9910)
- Apple GarageBand uses orange (not red) for flat/sharp. **[product doc]** — [GarageBand tuner](https://help.apple.com/garageband/mac/10.1.5/en.lproj/gbndb40183f2.html)
- Pardue & McPherson: students looked at the visual feedback only 16% of the time and said it "changed too rapidly". **[study]** — [Frontiers 2019](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.00627/full)

### Inferences
- For a miss cue in a practice loop: a 100–200 ms colour shift or a small shape change at the note's position (brief, precise, local, cancellable), no shake, no full-screen flash; a Reduce-Motion variant that is a pure colour/opacity change.
- Error-colour hierarchy that satisfies both NN/g and the "not aggressive" brief: keep saturated red for true errors (mic failed, song file broken), use amber/desaturated warm for "off target", and give the direction with geometry (needle left/right, arrow, tilt) plus one word.
- Haptics: Apple's "Error" notification pattern (four pulses) is noticeably harsher than "Warning" (two); a tuning/timing miss is a warning-class event at most, and a single light impact or selection tick is closer to the "noticed but not conscious" ideal. Since the app records with the microphone, haptics may bleed into the pitch detection (Apple's explicit caveat); test or default off while recording.
- A persistent needle/bar that is always moving is itself "motion"; damping and a dead zone are both an accuracy and an accessibility measure.

### Gaps
- Material Design 3 official pages (snackbar, banner, text-field error states, colour roles) could not be fetched; nothing in these notes quotes M3 directly.
- No official Google Material or Apple page specifically about error *colour* beyond "don't rely on colour alone" was retrieved.
- Apple HIG "Feedback" page (linked from Motion and Haptics) was not fetched.

## Key question 6: What copy patterns work for errors in a practice app?

### Takeaway
No study tests practice-app microcopy directly; the defensible pattern is assembled from NN/g (precise, non-blaming, no "invalid/wrong", no humour, remedy included), rhythm-game labels (single directional words: EARLY, LATE), tuner conventions (sharp/flat plus the physical action: tune down/up, tighten/loosen) and Duolingo's forewarning. Keep framing neutral and specific rather than either scolding or gushing (Gardner & Wood).

### Cited Findings
- NN/g: describe the exact problem, offer the remedy, avoid "invalid", "illegal", "incorrect", keep a positive nonjudgmental tone, no humour, no jargon. **[official guidance]** — [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/error-message-guidelines/)
- Apple haptic vocabulary models a tone ladder: Warning ("has produced a warning of some kind") vs Error ("an error has occurred") vs Retry ("an action failed but they can retry it"). **[official guidance]** — [Apple HIG Playing haptics](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics)
- Rhythm-game labels in use: EARLY / LATE / MISS (StepManiaX); PERFECT/GREAT/GOOD/OK/MEH/MISS (osu!mania); "LATE" and "MISSED" pop-ups (Rocksmith). **[wiki/forum]** — [StepManiaX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/StepManiaX); [osu!mania](https://osu.ppy.sh/wiki/en/Gameplay/Judgement/osu!mania); [Rocksmith Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/221680/discussions/0/541906989393368380)
- Tuner language: "flat"/"sharp" with direction of motion (Peterson), and consumer guides translate to the physical action: "whether you need to tighten or loosen the tuning peg". **[product doc/secondary]** — [Peterson](https://www.petersontuners.com/support/stroboSoft); [todoandroid GuitarTuna guide](https://en.todoandroid.es/guitar-tuna-app-android-guitar-tuner-ukulele-violin-free/)
- Duolingo's forewarning copy ("this is a hard exercise" in spirit) reduced the sting of mistakes. **[product doc]** — [Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-difficult-exercises/)
- Dweck: "not yet" as a grade label; praise process not talent. **[talk/claim]** — [Dweck transcript](https://singjupost.com/carol-s-dweck-the-power-of-yet-at-tedxnorrkoping-transcript/)
- Gardner & Wood: overly positive error framing hurt performance for high-self-efficacy users. **[study]** — [Massey](https://mro.massey.ac.nz/items/e7779b6c-4756-4112-acf1-c2ca9661e564/full)
- Pardue & McPherson: students often lacked note-name knowledge, so the display needs note names and clear correction cues; open question whether "impolite" highlighting motivates or repels. **[study]** — [Frontiers 2019](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.00627/full)

### Inferences (proposed copy, derived from the above, not tested)
- Pitch, in-flow (one or two words, next to the note): "sharp" / "flat"; with a direction glyph. With more room: "a bit sharp — tune down", "flat — tune up"; magnitude as "+12 c" only for players who opt into cents.
- Timing, in-flow: "early" / "late" (lowercase, small, fades in ~300–500 ms); no "miss" word unless outside the widest window, and then "missed" rather than "wrong".
- Consequence notice (transient, 3–5 s): "Tempo 80 → 72" or "Slowed to 72 bpm" with optional action "Keep 80"; forewarn instead where possible: "Faster part next".
- Session summary (where growth framing belongs, not in-flow): "Not yet at 100 bpm: 3 of 4 bars clean" rather than "Failed".
- Avoid: "Wrong", "Invalid", "Fail", streak-break shouts, exclamation marks on misses, jokes.

### Gaps
- No empirical test of specific practice-app microcopy ("a bit sharp" vs "sharp" vs "+12 cents") was found; the proposals above are inferences.
- Yousician, Simply Guitar and Fender Play in-app error wording was not retrieved.
